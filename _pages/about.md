Welcome to my researcher page! I am a postdoctoral researcher in [**Dr. Reza Abbasi-Asl's** lab](https://abbasilab.org/) as part of the UCSF Department of Neurology. I work on method development in transformer-based spatial transcriptomics algorithms, causal inference, and machine learning interpretability research with an emphasis on understanding driving factors of  neurodegenerative disease. I recently defended my **PhD in Human Genetics** at the University of Pittsburgh, where I worked for four years as a member of [**Dr. HJ Park's** lab](http://parklab.pitt.edu/members.html). I primarily focused on developing machine learning and causal inference methods to better understand the role of risk factors for complex diseases. Additionally, I worked on interpretability research to better understand how single cell foundation models perform in perturbation tasks. Please check out the papers and projects I've worked on in the **Published Work** tab. 
 
I received my M.S. in **Genetic Epidemiology** from the University of Washington, Seattle in June 2022, and my B.S. in **Genetics and Genomics** from the University of California, Davis along with a **minor in Statistics** in June 2020. I credit a lot of my scientific curiosity and appreciation for research to my time working in [**Dr. Megan Dennis's** lab](https://www.dennislab.org/) and the wonderful mentors I had as an undergrad. 

When I'm not on research duty, my hobbies include watching any and all sports, playing the piano and drums, playing basketball and tennis, nature photography, and all things coffee. 

I am incredibly passionate about teaching and mentorship, and believe it is incredibly important to share the knowledge and insights I have gained during my time as a researcher! If you have any questions about my work, or just want to chat about research, feel free to send me an email at: [aditya.sriram@ucsf.edu](mailto:aditya.sriram@ucsf.edu).
